# How It Felt - Speaker Script (about 2.5 minutes)

## Slide 1 - cover (15 seconds)
Hi, I'm Aitole. My project is called How It Felt. It's an interactive museum of ordinary experiences from 2026, built for the people of 3026. It's a website with three small exhibits, and the whole thing is already playable.

## Slide 2 - the idea (35 seconds)
The starting point was a frustration. Most messages to the future try to say something big, love, hope, warnings, and they all end up sounding the same. What actually disappears over a thousand years is not our big statements. It's the texture of being alive. People in 3026 will have our phones in their museums. They will never know what it felt like to wait eleven minutes for a reply and then receive "ok". So in this project, you don't read about the feeling. You have the feeling, briefly. Each exhibit is a small simulation, not a text.

## Slide 3 - the exhibits (45 seconds)
Three exhibits. The Typing Bubble is about waiting. You watch the dots, you can click to send another question mark, and it only makes the wait longer. One Percent is about battery anxiety. Every movement of your cursor drains the battery faster, so the only way to survive is to hold completely still. Rain on Glass is the quiet one. You drag to wipe the glass, and the rain keeps coming, because sometimes watching rain was a complete activity. These are all running live, these are the actual exhibits playing right now.

## Slide 4 - references (25 seconds)
Three references. Neal.fun showed me that a browser can be a museum and that you explain things by letting people do them. Pippin Barr's v r 3 is a gallery of ordinary water, and it taught me that the mundane becomes meaningful when you frame it as a collection. And the Museum of Broken Relationships is the physical version of my idea. Objects survive. What needs preserving is the feeling around them.

## Slide 5 - how it works (25 seconds)
Technically, it's p5.js. There's one shared Exhibit class, and each exhibit extends it with its own draw and interaction methods. Sound is oscillator blips for messages and filtered noise for rain. Next week I'm adding two more exhibits, the loading bar stuck at 99, and one more I want to choose with classmates during testing. Presentation on December 8.

## Slide 6 - closing (10 seconds)
That's it. The message to 3026 is: you had to be there, until now. The site is live at this link if anyone wants to feel it for themselves. Thank you.

## If they ask questions

- Why these three experiences? Because they're universal and tiny. Everyone in this room has lived all three, probably this week. That's exactly what makes them worth preserving.
- Is the interaction just a gimmick? No, it is the content. A description of waiting is information. Waiting is understanding. The piece only works if you feel a little impatient.
- Will there be more exhibits? Yes, the format is a collection, so it can grow. The loading bar at 99 is next.
- What about the time capsule? The box connects directly. I'm packing ordinary objects, a dead cable and a transit card, with no explanation, which is the whole thesis. Objects without feelings.
